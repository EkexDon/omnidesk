The desk

One window instead of eleven apps.

A mail client, a calendar, a Kanban board, a daily checklist, a word processor, a notes app, a voice recorder, a PDF annotator, a whiteboard, a slide editor and a music player — as one app, in panes you arrange yourself.

01 Write

Docs

A word processor on real pages.

Write on sheets of paper that paginate as you type — titles, headings, quotes, tables, images and checklists in four bundled typefaces.

  • Templates, version history and a distraction-free focus mode
  • Export to PDF, Word, Markdown or HTML; import .docx, Markdown and HTML
  • Drop a mail, a note or a PDF quote in and it arrives with a link back

Notes

Quick notes that link back.

A scratchpad that grows into a knowledge base: #tags, [[links]] that follow every rename, backlinks under each note and a graph of how it all connects.

  • Record voice memos straight into a note
  • Quick capture with ⇧⌘N — or ⇧⌘Space from anywhere on your Mac (on Windows Ctrl+Shift+N, and Ctrl+Shift+Space once you turn it on in Settings)
  • Markdown shortcuts, code blocks, tables and nested folders

02 Make

Whiteboard

An infinite canvas for the messy part.

  • Stickies, shapes, freehand ink, images and frames
  • Arrows that stay attached when you move things
  • Cards for anything on your desk — a mail, a task, a song that plays on the board
  • Smart guides, templates and PNG export

Slides

Decks, built from what you already made.

A slide sorter, themes, text that fits itself to its box and speaker notes. Present full screen with a presenter view on a second display.

  • Drop in a whiteboard frame, an image, or a task board as a chart that links back to it
  • Turn a document or note into a deck from its headings
  • Export to PDF, PowerPoint or PNG

03 Plan

Tasks

Kanban for projects, a checklist for today.

Boards with columns, WIP limits and checklists; a Today list that carries yesterday’s leftovers over. Type Call Sam tomorrow 3pm !high #work and the date, priority and label are set for you — in English or German.

Calendar

Your week at a glance.

Day, week, month and agenda. Drag to create, move and resize; repeating events; .ics import and export, calendar subscriptions and mail invitations you answer from the event.

04 Read & reply

Mail

Every inbox in one list.

A unified inbox for your IMAP accounts — iCloud, Gmail, Fastmail, Yahoo, GMX and others — with conversations, follow-up reminders and local search. Remote images stay blocked until you allow them.

PDF

Read with a pen in hand.

Highlights, underlines and sticky notes, find, outline and thumbnails. Take notes opens a note right beside the page, and the annotated PDF exports with your marks in it.

05 Listen

Music

A soundtrack for the work.

Your own music library — MP3, AAC, FLAC, WAV, Ogg and Opus — with playlists, smart lists, media keys and a 10-band equalizer over a live spectrum.

  • Jam sessions: friends anywhere join with a room code and hear what you play, live — and can save a song they like
  • Group soundtrack: one member DJs a shared queue and everyone in the group hears it softly while working

Group Workspaces

Work together, with no server in the middle.

A group workspace is a shared space for a team, a class or a family. It syncs directly between the members’ computers — Macs and Windows PCs alike — end-to-end encrypted, with no account and nothing stored on anyone’s server. Your Personal workspace never leaves your computer.

Local-first

Your data lives on your computer.

Mail, events, tasks, documents, notes, boards and decks are stored in a folder on your Mac or PC — no OmniDesk account, and nothing copied to a server of ours. Here is everything that goes over the network, and when.

When you add a mail account
OmniDesk talks to that provider’s IMAP and SMTP servers, on the sync interval you choose.
When you load images in a mail
The images of that message are fetched — only after you click Load images, allow the sender or choose to always load them. Never from your local network.
When you subscribe to a calendar
That feed’s address is fetched on the refresh interval you set.
When you’re in a group or a Jam
Public relays help the members’ computers find each other (by default the MQTT brokers of HiveMQ, EMQX and Eclipse Mosquitto) and STUN servers from Google and Cloudflare find a direct route. The content then travels encrypted, straight between those computers. A TURN relay is used only if you add one.
When you open or fetch a link
It goes where the link points, when you ask — web links open in your browser.
Everything else
Nothing. No analytics, no telemetry, no crash reports, no update pings, no account.

The same goes for this website: no cookies, no trackers, fonts served from here. Its only outside request asks GitHub for the latest version number.

On a Mac

  1. 1

    Download

    Get OmniDesk-Hub-mac-arm64.dmg and open it.

  2. 2

    Drag to Applications

    Drag OmniDesk Hub onto the Applications folder in the window that opens.

  3. 3

    Open it once

    macOS says it can’t verify the app. Click Done (Cancel on macOS 13–14) — that’s expected for now.

  4. 4

    Open Anyway

    System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, click Open Anyway next to OmniDesk Hub and confirm. Only the first time.

    If macOS says the app is damaged, use the Terminal fix below.

Why the warning? OmniDesk Hub isn’t notarized by Apple yet, so macOS can’t check who made it and asks you to confirm once. On macOS 13 and 14 you can also Control-click the app in Applications, choose Open and confirm.

Still won’t open, or macOS says the app “is damaged”?

That’s the download quarantine flag. Remove it for this one app in Terminal, then open OmniDesk Hub again:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/OmniDesk Hub.app"

On Windows

  1. 1

    Download

    Get OmniDesk-Hub-win-x64.exe (…-win-arm64.exe on Windows on Arm) and open it.

  2. 2

    More info → Run anyway

    If Windows says it protected your PC, click More info, then Run anyway. Only the first time.

  3. 3

    Install

    It installs for your account only — no administrator rights — and adds OmniDesk Hub to the Start menu. Tick Create a desktop shortcut at the end if you want one.

  4. 4

    Open it

    From the Start menu. Uninstalling (Settings → Apps) keeps your data unless you choose to delete it.

Why the warning? The installer isn’t signed with a code-signing certificate yet, so Microsoft Defender SmartScreen doesn’t know the publisher and asks you to confirm once.

FAQ

Fair questions.

Is it free?

Yes. OmniDesk Hub is free to download and use — no subscription, no account, no ads.

Is there a Windows version?

Yes — for Windows 10 and 11, on 64-bit PCs and on Windows on Arm. The download buttons pick the installer for your PC; it installs for your account without administrator rights, and a Mac and a Windows PC work together in one group. On the Mac: Apple Silicon with macOS 13 Ventura or later (if an Intel build is published, the buttons offer it automatically). There is no Linux version yet.

Why does macOS — or Windows — warn me the first time?

The app isn’t notarized by Apple or signed for Windows yet, so neither can verify the developer and asks you to confirm once — on a Mac Open Anyway, on Windows More info → Run anyway (see Install). After that it opens like any other app.

Where is my data, and how do I back it up?

Everything lives in ~/Library/Application Support/OmniDesk Hub — Settings → About shows the folder and reveals it in Finder. Time Machine includes it. To move to a new Mac, use Migration Assistant — it also brings the Keychain, where OmniDesk Hub keeps your mail passwords and group keys. If you copy the folder by hand, sign in to your mail accounts again and rejoin your groups with a new invite from a member. Your music stays in your own folders. On Windows the folder is %APPDATA%\OmniDesk Hub (Settings → About shows it in File Explorer); back it up like any folder. To move to a new PC, copy it, then sign in to your mail accounts again and rejoin your groups — Windows protects the passwords and keys for your account on that PC only.

How do I update?

OmniDesk Hub never checks for updates on its own. Download the latest version from this page or GitHub Releases. On a Mac, drag it over the old app in Applications; on Windows, run the new installer — it replaces the old version. Either way your data stays where it is.

Which mail accounts work?

Any IMAP and SMTP account that signs in with a password or an app password — iCloud, Gmail, Fastmail, Yahoo, GMX, WEB.DE or your own server. Sign-in through a browser (OAuth) isn’t supported yet, so work accounts that only allow that can’t connect.

Do the others in my group need an account?

No. Everyone installs OmniDesk Hub and joins with the invite code or file. There is no sign-up and no server that stores your group’s content.

Is OmniDesk Hub open source?

No. It’s free to use, but the source code isn’t public — all rights reserved. Releases are published on GitHub.
